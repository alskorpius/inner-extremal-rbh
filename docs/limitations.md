# Limitations

Boundaries of the results reported in *Inner-extremal regular black holes with NEC-satisfying anisotropic sources*. Read this before reusing any number from the repository.

## Scope of the claim

Two statements are asserted. Existence: static spherically symmetric metrics of the class `g_tt g_rr = -1`, sourced by an anisotropic vacuum-like tensor with `T^t_t = T^r_r`, admit a degenerate (triple-root) inner horizon with `kappa_- = 0` while satisfying the null and weak energy conditions everywhere, for `lambda = l/M` between about 0.02 and 1.066, the upper end being a quadruple root rather than a Reissner-Nordström-type double root. Absence: in classical spherically symmetric general relativity no mechanism selects or protects that degeneracy.

The second statement is not a theorem. It is the conjunction of separately closed mechanism classes, each with its own premises: profile tuning, mass inflation as feedback, semiclassical flux at the inner horizon, a protecting conserved quantity, the extremal endpoint as attractor, smooth Lagrangian sources in Kantowski-Sachs minisuperspace, bulk viscosity, and a first-order phase transition. Mechanisms outside these classes are not excluded. The paper claims neither stability of the configuration, nor uniqueness of the final state, nor a derivation of the profile or of `l ∝ M` from an action. Non-spherical perturbations of the de Sitter plateau and of the T-region were never computed in any realisation of the source.

## Model assumptions

Units are `G = c = 1` with `M = 1`. The class `g_tt g_rr = -1` is equivalent to `T^t_t = T^r_r`, where the transverse null energy condition collapses to `rho + p_perp = -(R/2) rho' >= 0`, that is, to `rho' <= 0`. Energy-condition statements here are statements about monotone density and nothing more.

The family is constructed, not derived. The logarithmic slope `sigma = -d ln rho / d ln R` is parameterised by sigmoids plus a Gaussian dip with eight parameters (base values `u1 = 1`, `u2 = 4`, `u3 = 8`, `w1 = 0.25`, `w2 = 0.35`, `w3 = 0.1`, `s_end = 24`, `sigma_min = 1`), the triple root imposed by solving for the remaining slope parameter. Non-monotone `sigma` is the only route to a triple root in this class. No action yields this profile together with `l ∝ M`: nonlinear electrodynamics with a magnetic monopole reproduces the geometry from a four-dimensional action but fixes `l = const`, and scalar-plus-NED constructions, local scale-free response laws and a gravitating monopole defect all fail. So `lambda_max = 1.066` bounds a hand-chosen parameterisation, not the class; the only rigorous bound is `lambda <= R_-/M < 2`.

## Numerical limitations

The family constants `lambda = 0.271016`, `R_- = 0.677630`, `R_+ = 1.984648`, `kappa_+ = 0.221082` are reliable to four significant digits, fixed by the merging of stationary points on a logarithmic grid of 60001 points with step `2.9e-4`. The boundary `lambda_max = 1.066` is reliable to three. Symbolic identification returns formal closed forms for each, but also matches ten random control numbers at comparable complexity, so none was counted.

The triple root is numerical. The base profile has `kappa_- = -5.4e-8` and `f''(R_-) = 2.5e-3`, and on a dense grid `f` changes sign three times between `R = 0.677435` and `R = 0.677811`, with `|f| <= 1.4e-12` between the zeros. Read `kappa_- = 0` as zero to within `1e-7`.

One calculation is stochastic. The family-boundary search draws 300 random parameter points from a generator seeded with 7, then runs Nelder-Mead from the best. It reproduces exactly at that seed and to three digits at others, so `lambda_max` is a best-found value and the true maximum of this parameterisation may be slightly higher. Everything else is deterministic, depending on library versions only through integrator tolerances; those were not varied, so integrator error is unquantified beyond the four-digit grid statement.

Quasinormal-mode shifts carry two error scales. The time-domain calculation has a systematic error near `3e-4` in damping against a Leaver control; the frequency-domain Wronskian method reaches `1e-6`. They agree to `7e-5` in frequency and `4.2e-4` in damping, with matching signs. The `l = 2` axial band (frequency `-0.95%` to `+0.51%`, damping `-4.2%` to `+0.14%`) is meaningful at that width, but an individual damping shift of order `1e-4` sits at time-domain noise level and should be taken from the frequency-domain calculation.

## Physical restrictions

Semiclassics use the two-dimensional Polyakov tensor in the Unruh state, giving `T_uu(R_-) = (kappa_+^2 - kappa_-^2)/(48 pi) = +3.24e-4` and a luminosity of `1.4e-29` W for 10 solar masses against the `2.3e31` W required, a ratio of `6e-61` on a timescale of `1e73` s. The four-dimensional regularity condition may differ, and the finding that semiclassical feedback drives `kappa_-` toward `kappa_+` rather than zero rests on the two-dimensional coefficient structure. The layer thickness where infalling energy density reaches core density, `2.7e-10` m for 10 solar masses, uses the same `1/f^2` formula as elsewhere and is not an independent check.

Mass inflation is treated in the Ori model. The feedback calculation iterates static profile to Ori model and back rather than solving self-consistent dynamics, and is prescription-dependent: one prescription destroys the trapped region after a single substitution and is discarded as an artefact, while the retained prescription and its smoothed variant agree in sign across all twenty series. With a Price tail of `1e-3` and `p = 11` the exponential phase is reached numerically only for `|kappa_-|` above about 0.5; below that the run stops near an e-folding count of `1e-3` and continues with the Ori asymptotic relation, which the logs flag.

Viscosity is handled by linearisation at the exit, `s^2 - 8 pi zeta s + 8 pi rho w = 0`, plus a scan of roughly 300 runs over `zeta = zeta_0 rho^n` in Eckart and Israel-Stewart forms. Zero degenerate exits there is an existence check, not an impossibility proof. At the extremal end, the Aretakis instability of degenerate horizons is carried as a literature caveat; the primary source was not verified against its text.

The halo mass fraction is not bounded below by the class. The quoted `0.77%` of the base profile follows only from a curvature heuristic requiring the cut-off curvature below about 2.4 times its trigger value; relax that and the smooth family reaches halos under `1e-5`, with ringdown shifts falling to zero in proportion.

A static thin wall needs `f > 0` on both sides at the wall radius, impossible for `l < 2M`. Static walls exist only in the gravastar regime `l >~ 2M`, where the object has no horizon, and those equilibria at `R* = 2.99` to `5.81 M` are radially unstable, `V'' = -0.047` to `-0.567`, requiring `Gamma > Gamma_c = 6.65`, `0.87` or `0.36` to stabilise. The junction analysis assumes an infinitely thin timelike shell with Israel conditions and a barotropic surface equation of state.

The NED realisation is linearly stable outside the horizon (`det S > 0`, `l = 2` to `4`), but inside it the principal-symbol eigenvalue turns negative over the band `[0.90, 1.63]` and in the core, with amplification `ln||T|| = 2.4 l`, so the interior evolution problem is not Hadamard well-posed. That is a property of the Lagrangian rather than the geometry, but interior perturbation statements use a matter model known to be inadequate there, and no non-NED Lagrangian for the layer exists.

The polar core instability is unbounded at fixed `l` only where the complete centre coefficient `-(p+2)/4 [2l(l+1) - (p+4)]` lies below `-1/4`. That holds at every `l >= 2` for plateau index `p <= 7`, which covers every profile used here. For flatter plateaus the lowest multipoles have bounded growth instead (`p = 10`, `l = 2`: `26.6/M`) while higher `l` still fall to the centre. A C-infinity non-analytic plateau edge was not computed. Versions up to v1.1.1 of this package quoted only the `l(l+1)` part of the coefficient; the numbers were unaffected because the solver always used the full potential.

The Kantowski-Sachs no-go assumes an autonomous system, a smooth Lagrangian source and uniqueness of solutions; the first-order-transition result assumes Kantowski-Sachs homogeneity, spherical symmetry, and an instantaneous or single-relaxation-time transition. The holographic argument uses the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy of the outer horizon, established for stationary horizons, and carries it inward with Bousso's covariant bound, a conjecture; the volumetric count against it is kinematic, counting Planck cells rather than dynamical degrees of freedom, and concerns the scaling `M^3` against `M^2`, not prefactors.

Inhomogeneous collapse uses Lemaître-Tolman-Bondi dust with an effective mass function. All 160 shells bounce at `K = (0.98 +/- 0.01) x 24/l^4` for amplitudes `0.01` to `0.3` of both signs, but afterwards the shells cross and the weak solution beyond the caustic is not unique, both prescriptions tested proving inconsistent; nothing about post-caustic dynamics is a result. For accretion, the tracking law `l = lambda M` is imposed rather than derived, the response time `tau_r <~ 0.1 M` is a parameter, and its alternative, outgoing emission accumulating near the inner horizon instead of a negative-energy influx, was not checked for self-consistency.

## Rotation

Rotation is a caveat, not a result. Every rotating quasinormal-mode and eikonal number quoted refers to the Franzin et al. metric, a conformally regular class with `m(0) != 0` that keeps the Kerr ring at `r = 0` and obtains regularity from a conformal factor. It has no de Sitter core and is not the rotating analogue of the static scenario. Its explicit conformal factor was not reproduced here, and the regularity of its Kretschmann scalar at the centre was not evaluated in this work.

For the Gürses-Gürsey class carrying the scenario's own `m ∝ r^3`, spin removes the triple root: `kappa_- ∝ a^{4/3}` at small spin, reaching `-0.093` at `a = 0.1` and `-0.20` at `a = 0.3`, and the base profile has no horizon at all for `a >~ 0.58`. Retuning restores degeneracy through three conditions on three shape parameters, hence codimension two, but only for `a <~ 0.1` to `0.15` and not for every shape: two of those tested already fail at `a = 0.1`. A rotating triple root with a de Sitter core at astrophysical spin was not constructed.

Ring curvature was evaluated only for a polynomial core, where the Ricci scalar is `12/l^2` on the equator and zero off it, and the Kretschmann scalar `24/l^4` on the equator and zero at other polar angles, with intermediate values along directions approaching the ring: finite, but directionally discontinuous. Not established are the energy conditions of the Gürses-Gürsey class at nonzero spin, the curvature structure for a non-polynomial mass function, and the continuation to negative `r`. The maximal spin found limits this one shape family; whether it limits the construction in general was not tested.

## Known technical failures

Three runs failed, and none of their output enters the paper. The gas-hydrodynamics settling calculation, an explicit scheme, breaks down at `t = 97.9`; what survives from that directory is the separate static result that Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff configurations exist only for `M <= 1.30 l`. The symbolic Kretschmann computation for the full rational mass function in the rotating case did not complete and was stopped after forty minutes, replaced by a polynomial core, so the ring-curvature values above hold for that core only. In the viscosity scan at `n = 0` with positive `zeta_0`, some trajectories leave the physical domain and reach negative density near the outer horizon; this is the known Eckart pathology, the viscous term diverging at any non-degenerate horizon, and those runs count as non-degenerate exits rather than physical solutions. For the same reason the smeared first-order transition with a heavy fluid degenerates numerically at the turning point and yields no degenerate exit by construction rather than by dynamics.

## What would falsify or strengthen the conclusion

The negative conclusion would be overturned by a source outside the closed classes, dissipative and non-Lagrangian in the Kantowski-Sachs variables or with a non-smooth right-hand side, that makes the degenerate exit an attractor while producing positive entropy, non-negative interior mass, and a triple rather than double root. Such an attractor already exists mathematically: the anti-dissipative window at `n = 0` with `zeta_0` between `-0.3` and `-0.07` lands every trajectory on a degenerate horizon at `b* = 1.44` to `1.72 M` with the null energy condition satisfied along the path. It fails on every physical count, giving negative entropy production, a double root with a jump in `f''`, and a negative central mass. A physical realisation of the same feedback would change the conclusion.

A conserved quantity, or a topological or supersymmetric saturation condition, fixing the three triple-root conditions as a relation among constants of the theory rather than between `M` and `l`, would do the same. Known protected degenerate horizons do not: extremal Reissner-Nordström and the NED realisation both tie degeneracy to a charge-mass relation that accretion breaks, and the global monopole fixes the scale by a coupling constant, leaving that scale not proportional to the mass.

The conclusion is strengthened most directly by a four-dimensional stress tensor in place of the two-dimensional semiclassical estimate, which removes the largest single premise behind closure of the semiclassical channel. Also valuable: a non-spherical perturbation analysis of the plateau and the T-region, the one part of the classical problem never examined, and a parameterisation-independent characterisation of the extremal endpoint, which would replace the three-digit `lambda_max` with a bound.
